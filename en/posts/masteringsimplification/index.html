Overview of Simplification#

Simplification is a core practice in the era of Moore’s Law. It emphasizes improving efficiency, focusing on critical issues, and discovering more valuable solutions in complex and dynamic environments.

Part One: Classic Simplification#

1. Focus on the Primary Contradiction#

Core Philosophy#

Select “the most important issue” from many and focus on solving it rather than spreading attention too thin.

  • Pitfalls of Multiple Objectives:
    1. Chasing Ease, Ignoring Importance:
      • “Ease” is viewed from a personal perspective, while “importance” is evaluated from a third-party value perspective.
    2. Weakened Competitiveness:
      • Fragmented input results in inefficiency, even for simple problems.
    3. Failure of Delegation:
      • Fragmented tasks reduce team effectiveness, leading to inadequate delegation.
    4. Traps of Indecision:
      • Poor judgment of core values leads to unnecessary attachment and ineffective trade-offs.

2. Solve No Difficult Problems, Win First, Fight Later#

(1) Stay Away from Problems#

Most problems yield insufficient benefits to justify the costs. Blindly “solving problems” often traps people in a low-value loop.

  • Core Principles:
    • 90% of problems are low-value, with half being negative-value.
    • Learn to avoid problems and focus on value addition and high-impact work.

(2) Avoid Being Forced#

When under pressure, decision-making and execution quality significantly decline, often resulting in cascading errors.

  • Manifestations of Murphy’s Law:
    1. Tasks take longer than expected.
    2. Problems occur at the most inopportune times.
    3. Poor decisions create a chain of negative outcomes.

(3) Wait for Conditions to Favor You#

Observe the market and enter at the most favorable window.

  • Market Extremes:
    • Dimensional Reduction: Short-term factors force participants out.
    • Dimensional Elevation: Long-term certainty leads to extreme market trends.

3. Eliminate Complexity and Perfectionism#

(1) Eliminate Complexity#

  • Follow the “KISS” principle (Keep It Simple, Stupid).
  • Remove redundancies and minimize resource wastage.

(2) Eliminate Perfectionism#

  • Perfectionism often leads to failure.
  • Consistency and steady progress are more critical than aiming for instant perfection.

Part Two: Simplification in the Era of Moore’s Law#

1. Extreme Chip-level Simplification#

Chip technology relies on massive repetition and replication, making simplification vital for efficiency.

  • Benefits of Simplification:
    1. Reduces friction and improves production yield.
    2. Controls costs to achieve competitive pricing.

2. Addition Equals Liability#

In the Moore’s Law era, every added step magnifies costs and defects, making simplification essential.

  • Engineering Simplification:
    • Reduce steps and components to minimize long-term complexity and financial burden.

3. Make Time Your Ally#

Time is a critical variable in tumultuous waves. Simplification helps turn time into an ally rather than an obstacle.

  • Strength vs. Weakness Thinking:
    • Strength Thinking (“Filling the Blanks”): Adds content but risks complexity.
    • Weakness Thinking (“Removing the Blanks”): Simplifies the process, reducing pressure.

4. Take a Long-Term View#

Use “psychological distance” to reassess the future and improve decision-making.

  • Significance of Psychological Distance:
    • Abstracting factors like time, space, and social connections helps make decisions more rational.
    • Shift focus to long-term resource allocation.

5. Tenfold Resources for Half the Work#

Concentrate resources on core tasks. Achieve amplified effects through efficient simplification.

  • Methodology:
    1. Redefine the task.
    2. Combine with simplification principles to achieve higher resource returns.

6. Immune Systems for Tasks#

Simplification must be preceded by questioning and removing unnecessary tasks.

  • Question Before Deletion:
    • Especially for ideas from “smart people,” which often appear reasonable but may not be necessary.

Part Three: Practices and Pitfalls in Simplification#

1. Rebuilding Mental Strength#

Mental strength determines resilience in the face of complexity, allowing individuals to handle failure and grow.

  • Traits of Mental Strength:
    • Recovery: Quickly bouncing back from setbacks.
    • Growth Mindset: Viewing failures as learning opportunities.

2. Warning Signs#

(1) Grand Narratives#

Avoid using unrealistic “stories” to cover up the true complexity of issues. Rational analysis is better than blind adherence.

(2) Hindsight Bias#

  • Verifying problems is easier than solving them. Overemphasis on “validation after the fact” wastes resources.

Part Four: Advanced Simplification in the Era of Moore’s Law#

1. The Future of Violent Computational Leverage#

Advances in AI and computational power offer new possibilities for simplification.

  • Core Concepts:
    • Use computational force to unlock higher-dimensional information.
    • Transform “search” into “generation.”

2. Rebuilding for Tenfold Returns#

Simplify to identify the most critical issues and redesign solutions with a tenfold perspective.

  • Elon Musk’s Principle:
    • “Tenfold challenges are not necessarily tenfold harder.”

Conclusion#

Simplification is not just a technical philosophy but a core strategy for value creation. By simplifying, individuals and organizations can maximize outcomes with minimal resources.

Chairman Mao’s evaluation of simplification’s personality: “Unselfish, decisive, focusing on major issues, empowering others, grasping clearly, calculating thoroughly, completing tasks, letting go when needed.”
